## Three reasons the spare PC goes back in the box

- **Nothing has to be switched off first.** Mac software and PC software are open together, side by side, on
  the same display. The moment where you save everything, shut down and wait for another logo to appear is
  simply removed from the day, and with it the small tax that stops people using the other system at all.
- **The partition you already built is not thrown away.** An existing Boot Camp installation can be carried
  across and turned into a virtual machine instead of being rebuilt from an ISO. Applications, settings and
  activations survive the trip, which is the difference between an evening's work and an afternoon's coffee.
- **Setup is a choice from a short menu, not a page of sliders.** Say whether the machine is for productivity,
  games, design or development, and the settings and performance are tuned for you in one move. Anybody's
  first manual guess at memory and processor allocation is worse than the profile written for the job.

## Two desktops sharing one display

There are two honest ways to look at a guest system, and this one lets you pick per session. Hide it
completely and only its programs appear, floating in their own windows among your Mac applications, so a
colleague looking over your shoulder cannot tell which is which. Or leave it visible with its own background
and controls intact, which is what you want when the task is administering that system rather than using one
program inside it.

Most people end up alternating without thinking about it. The invisible arrangement suits the accountant who
needs one ledger tool; the visible one suits the person installing drivers, adjusting policies or teaching
somebody else where a setting lives.

## It is not only about Windows

The guest list is wider than the reputation suggests. Windows 10, 8.1 and 7 are the usual reason people
arrive, but Linux distributions, macOS itself and Google Chrome all run the same way — each in its own
window, each on top of the system you booted into. That matters for two groups in particular: developers who
want a throwaway Linux box that cannot damage anything, and anyone who needs an older Mac environment kept
reachable long after the hardware that ran it was recycled.

## Which edition, without reading a comparison chart

The base licence is defined by what it runs, and for one Windows program on a home Mac the answer stops
there. Pro Edition is defined by how much of your hardware a single guest may claim: as much as 64 GB of
vRAM and sixteen virtual processors, which is bought against a heavy workload rather than a passing need.
Business Edition is defined by who holds the paperwork — one volume key, licences administered centrally,
and the extra security controls a fleet owner is required to demonstrate. Household, engineer, IT department:
the three tiers map onto those three people almost exactly.
